GTM Systems Audit

An end-to-end review of your marketing and revenue tech stack. I map how data actually flows from first touch to closed revenue, flag the breakage points, and produce a phased current-state vs. future-state plan with a funnel view.

Enrichment & Vendor Waterfall Audit

Most enrichment stacks are bought by the seat and audited by nobody. Teams pay per record for several data vendors plus separate phone and email waterfalls, and nobody can say which of them actually changed a field in the CRM last month. I answer that per vendor, against your real records.

I trace the build, not the documentation. I rebuild the dependency graph from the live configuration (every formula, run condition, and field mapping) and compare it to what the design doc claims. On a recent engagement, none of the ten documented pipeline steps survived that comparison. The doc said a provenance check protected every phone lookup. In fact seven of the ten phone sources were checked, and the first three, the ones most likely to fire, read straight from the vendor payload unchecked.

I measure contribution, not coverage. Vendor dashboards report match rates, which is not the same as impact. I ran 1,004 records through a five-vendor email waterfall and compared the final verdict to the single validation check that ran first. They matched one to one: the five vendors changed the outcome zero times. Along the way they had correctly found 21 real typo corrections, which the formula threw away because it only credited a vendor whose answer exactly matched the submitted address.

I find the writes that never land. Enrichment that reaches the CRM and gets silently overwritten is worse than enrichment that fails, because it looks like it worked. Examples I've found: a company-size field overwritten by a before-save trigger, a country value that never cascades once the ISO field is set, and 29,172 accounts showing zero web traffic because a formula took MAX() across four blank inputs.

Deliverables: a column-level map of the enrichment pipeline with each vendor's real contribution rate measured against your records, a ranked list of dead spend (vendor output that never reaches a field, or reaches one that gets overwritten), the gaps between your documentation and your build written up as corrections, and a remediation list ordered by effort that separates "rebuild this" from "move three references."

Stack patterns I work with: Salesforce, Clay, ZoomInfo, Cognism, LeanData, BigQuery, Hightouch, ZeroBounce, Trestle. A typical engagement runs two to three weeks, and most of it is reading. Most findings aren't a system failing loudly. They're a documented guarantee and the deployed behavior drifting apart, producing plausible data nobody had reason to question.

Partnerships & Channel Systems

Partner data is the messiest schema in B2B: one account can be sourced by a referral partner, influenced by an SI, transacted through a reseller or cloud marketplace, and integrated with three tech partners, all at the same time. I design end-to-end partner systems that hold up to that, from the first application to the last payout, and that grow with the program instead of ahead of it.

Every partner type. Referral, reseller / VAR and distribution, technology and integration, SI / agency, and cloud marketplace partners each need different data, incentives, and workflows. I model them on one foundation so a partner can hold several roles without duplicate records or double-counted credit.

Data model, hierarchy & hygiene. A partner account model with parent / child hierarchies across regions, subsidiaries, and distributor-to-reseller tiers; contacts tied to the right level; and partner-on-opportunity relationships that capture role and credit. Then the hygiene work most programs skip: deduping applications against existing accounts, filtering out unqualified or duplicate sign-ups, and running new partners through an enrichment waterfall so firmographics, segment, and fit are scored before anyone approves them.

Portals, onboarding & content. A partner portal for self-serve onboarding, deal registration, lead distribution, and enablement, with role-based access, sharing rules, and user lifecycle governance so access stays clean as partners churn. Content management and certification tracks keep collateral current and tie enablement to tier status.

Deal registration & co-sell. Registration flows with clear approval, conflict, and expiry rules, channel-conflict resolution against direct deals, and account-mapping and co-sell signals routed to reps like any other buying signal, so overlap with partners becomes pipeline instead of friction.

Attribution. Sourced vs. influenced vs. transacted credit, defined once and applied consistently across the CRM, the warehouse, and partner reporting, with the rules finance, sales, and the partner team agree to before anyone gets paid on them.

Money flows. Commission and referral-fee calculation, rebates, partner payouts and payment processing, MDF and co-op fund requests, approvals, and claims, plus cloud marketplace listings and private offers wired into billing and revenue recognition.

Legal & compliance. Partner agreements and terms tracked against the record (tier, territory, discount and margin limits, renewal dates), tax and payee onboarding, data-sharing and privacy terms for account mapping and lead exchange, and the audit trail finance needs for partner spend.

Staged to maturity. Early on, keep it out of the CRM: track partners in a lightweight workspace and sync only the minimum fields. Add the CRM layer once partners source real pipeline, and the portal, payouts, and marketplace once volume justifies them. The goal is a program where every partner touch is credited correctly, paid correctly, and visible to the rep who needs it.
